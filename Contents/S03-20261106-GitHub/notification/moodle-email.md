Part 1: GitHub Beyond the Code — From a Fork to Your First Merged PR

Have you ever wondered how you could safely contribute your changes to a project like our weekly session repo, or even big projects like VS Code, whose authors you have never met? And why do people keep chasing those green squares on their profiles?

In this part, Siyuan He (Robert) will walk everyone through the standard workflow of open-source collaboration — entirely in the browser, with no installation and no command line. You will file a real Issue, fork a repository and create a branch, open your very first Pull Request, watch GitHub Actions (a robot colleague) check your changes automatically, and publish the repository as a live website with GitHub Pages.

By the end of the session, everyone will have a merged pull request — and a fresh green square on their profile.

Part 2: Getting Started in Research: Lessons from Research and Internships

If you are an undergraduate interested in trying research, where should you start? Why pursue research rather than something else?

In this session, I will draw on my research and internship experiences in academic labs and industry to discuss how to conduct research, how to think through problems, and how these experiences have helped me understand research and explore possible directions. The session will also cover communicating with supervisors and collaborators, understanding your research contributions, and finding your own pace amid peer pressure. Everyone is welcome to join!

Speaker Bio

Jiayi Zhang is a third-year Mathematics and Applied Mathematics student at the University of Nottingham Ningbo China. He is currently a research assistant at Tsinghua University’s Institute for Interdisciplinary Information Sciences and an intern with the embodied foundation model pretraining team at Meituan’s Robotics Research Institute. He previously worked as a research assistant at the IWIN-FINS Laboratory in the Department of Automation at Shanghai Jiao Tong University and EEE IoT at the University of Hong Kong. He also served as a Peer Mentor during the 2025–26 academic year.
